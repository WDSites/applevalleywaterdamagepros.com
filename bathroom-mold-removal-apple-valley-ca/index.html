Bathroom Mold Removal in Apple Valley, CA: DIY or Professional

By the Apple Valley Water Damage Pros team

Mold on grout and caulk is often a homeowner job. Mold behind tile or in drywall is not. Here is how to tell which one you have.

☎ Call (760) 769-1159

Bathroom mold removal comes up in almost every Apple Valley home sooner or later. The room is warm, damp after showers and full of porous materials such as caulk, grout and painted drywall. Most of the time, the mold is on the surface and can be handled by a homeowner. Sometimes it is not.

This guide separates the small jobs from the larger ones. It focuses on walls, tub surrounds, grout and caulk, which is different from mold on the ceiling covered in our other bathroom guide. If you see signs of a leak behind the wall, skip to the section on when to call.

What a homeowner can clean

The U.S. Environmental Protection Agency says that if the moldy area is less than about 10 square feet, in most cases you can handle the job yourself, and that hard surfaces can be scrubbed with detergent and water and dried completely. That covers most grout, tile and fixtures. Caulk is different, because it is porous and often holds growth below the surface. Removing and replacing it is usually better than cleaning it.

  1. Open a window or run the fan and keep the area ventilated.
  2. Remove old caulk and discard it.
  3. Scrub hard surfaces with detergent and water.
  4. Dry thoroughly with a towel and fan.
  5. Check the wall behind for softness or discoloration.
  6. Re-caulk with a mildew-resistant product once everything is fully dry.

Do not paint or caulk over moldy surfaces. The EPA says paint applied over moldy surfaces is likely to peel, and sealing mold in only hides it.

Signs the mold has moved behind the wall

  • Tile that is loose, cracked or sounds hollow
  • Drywall near the tub that is soft, stained or bulging
  • A musty smell that stays after surface cleaning
  • Mold that returns within weeks of cleaning
  • Dark staining that spreads upward from the floor or outward from the tub
  • A stain on the ceiling or wall of the room below

Any of these points to moisture getting into the structure. At that stage, scrubbing the surface does not help. The wall may need to be opened, dried and rebuilt, which is where water damage repair comes in. A moisture assessment shows how far it has gone.

What professional removal looks like

A crew sets plastic containment and negative air, so spores do not travel through the house. Affected drywall and backer board come out, and the framing is cleaned and dried. The ANSI/IICRC S520 standard guides this kind of remediation, and it is reasonable to ask any company whether it works to it.

After the structure reads dry, the wall is rebuilt with moisture-resistant materials and the shower is waterproofed and tested. If the source was a plumbing leak, water mitigation may have to dry the structure before remediation starts.

Apple Valley local note: hiring and permits

If you hire for the rebuild, check the license. The Contractors State License Board lets you look up a license online at cslb.ca.gov, and it advises written contracts that spell out materials, cleanup and payment schedule. It also advises asking about general liability insurance.

Shower rebuilds that move plumbing or change the structure may require a permit. The Town of Apple Valley Building and Safety Division handles plan review and inspection through the Permit Center, so ask what applies to your scope. For a bathroom in Adelanto or Victorville, ask the city instead.

Mention the home's ZIP code, 92307 or 92308, when you call a trade, since service areas often follow it.

Choosing materials for a bathroom rebuild

If a wall had to be opened, the rebuild is the time to choose materials that handle moisture better. Use moisture-resistant backer board behind tile, and a continuous waterproofing layer on the shower walls and pan. Silicone caulk at corners and edges stays flexible and resists staining longer than many alternatives.

Seal grout after it has cured, and reseal it on a schedule that the product recommends. Replace a weak exhaust fan while the ceiling is accessible. These choices are cheaper than a second tear-out.

Ask the installer to explain how the waterproofing was done and how it was tested. A good installer can show photos taken before the tile went on.

Renters, condos and shared walls

If you rent, report bathroom mold to your landlord in writing and keep a copy. Do not open walls or replace materials without permission, since the building belongs to someone else. If you own a condo, check whether the association is responsible for shared plumbing or walls before you hire anyone.

Water that comes from a neighbor's unit may involve their insurer as well as yours, so photograph the damage and note the dates. A restoration company can document the moisture and the source, which makes the conversation easier for everyone.

Keeping the bathroom dry afterward

  • Run the exhaust fan during showers and afterward
  • Squeegee tile and glass after use when practical
  • Hang towels so they dry and do not sit damp on the floor
  • Fix drips, slow drains and loose fixtures quickly
  • Check the caulk twice a year

No product keeps a bathroom clear of mold for good, because moisture is the driver. A bathroom that dries quickly after use is a bathroom that holds up.

Frequently asked questions

Is bathroom mold dangerous?

Questions about health effects belong with a physician. The EPA advises consulting a health professional before starting cleanup if you have health concerns.

Can I just re-caulk over mold?

No. Remove the old caulk first and clean the surface. Sealing over mold hides it and it usually returns.

How often should caulk be replaced?

When it cracks, peels or stains. Check it twice a year, and replace it sooner if it keeps blackening.

What if mold returns right after cleaning?

That suggests moisture inside the wall or poor ventilation. A moisture reading finds out which.

Does my 92307 or 92308 address change who I call for permits?

Both ZIP codes are in the Town of Apple Valley, so the Building and Safety Division applies. Properties just outside town limits may fall under San Bernardino County, so confirm your jurisdiction.
