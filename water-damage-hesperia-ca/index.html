How the age of your Hesperia home shapes the leak

A leak in a 1970s tract house and a leak in a house built in the last twenty years rarely start the same way. The age of your plumbing and roofing decides where water is likely to appear first. That is a general building fact, not a claim about any single street, so use it as a checklist for your own home.

  • Older homes may have galvanized steel or early copper lines that corrode from the inside and pinhole at joints.
  • Homes plumbed between the late 1970s and the mid 1990s may contain polybutylene pipe, which can fail at fittings without warning.
  • Newer homes often have PEX lines and plastic fittings that fail at crimp rings or when a fixture connector is overtightened.
  • Any age of home can have a failed water heater, a tired washer hose or a clogged condensate line.

Check your water heater label for its date, and look at the hose behind your washing machine. If the hose has a rubber jacket and you cannot remember when it was installed, replace it before it replaces your flooring.
