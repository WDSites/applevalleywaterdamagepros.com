Does House Insurance Cover Water Damage? A Guide for Apple Valley, CA

By the Apple Valley Water Damage Pros team

Coverage for water damage depends on how the water got in. This guide explains the common distinctions and the steps to take after a loss.

☎ Call (760) 769-1159

Whether house insurance covers water damage is the first question most homeowners ask, and the answer depends on how the water entered and what your policy says. In Apple Valley, as elsewhere in California, the usual dividing line is between sudden and accidental events and slow or neglected ones.

This guide explains those distinctions in general terms. It is not legal or insurance advice and we do not approve claims, so read your policy and ask your insurer about your situation.

The three common categories

Type of lossOften treated asNotes
Sudden and accidental (burst pipe, failed hose, appliance overflow)Often coveredPolicy language and exclusions still apply
Gradual or long-term leaks, seepage, neglectOften not coveredInsurers may cite maintenance exclusions
Flood (rising water from outside, storm runoff)Usually excludedNeeds separate flood insurance
Sewer or drain backupVariesMay need an endorsement

The California Department of Insurance describes flood insurance as covering events not usually covered by standard homeowners insurance, and it notes that flood insurance usually does not cover water damage from inside the home, such as burst pipes. That points to burst pipes being a homeowners-policy question and flood being a flood-policy question.

What to do in the first day

  1. Stop the water. Shut off the main valve if needed.
  2. Make temporary repairs to prevent further damage, such as covering a roof opening with a tarp.
  3. Photograph and video the damage and the source before moving anything.
  4. Call your insurer or agent to report the loss, and start a written log of every call and letter.
  5. Keep damaged items until the adjuster has seen them, unless they are unsafe.
  6. Keep receipts for mitigation, temporary repairs and any extra living costs.

The California Department of Insurance advises policyholders to make temporary repairs to prevent further damage, and it warns that a policy may not cover ensuing damage if you have not taken reasonable steps to secure the property.

Living costs and your timeline

If water makes your home unusable, the Department notes that policies may include additional living expenses and that you should receive a separate check for them. Ask your insurer what is covered, what receipts they need and how long it lasts.

Insurers also have deadlines. The Department says the Fair Claims Settlement Practices Regulations set time deadlines for insurers, and the details are on its website. Your policy may set its own deadlines for you to report and document a claim, so read the claims section early.

Apple Valley local note: sewer and septic backup

Backup coverage is worth checking in this part of the High Desert because homes differ in how they drain. The Town of Apple Valley utilities element says the Town runs the sewer collection system and that roughly 30 percent of development is connected to it, with the rest on on-lot septic systems. Ask your insurer how a backup from either is treated under your policy.

Storm-related losses are the other local wrinkle. Tropical Storm Hilary in 2023 brought torrential rain to mountain and desert communities across Southern California according to the National Weather Service, and a roof leak from a storm is a different claim from groundwater coming under a door.

If your home is in Hesperia or Victorville, the insurance rules are the same California rules, but the sewer and water arrangements differ by city.

How a restoration crew supports your claim

A crew cannot decide coverage, but it can make your claim easier to review. Typical documentation includes the date and time the loss was reported, photos of the damage and source, moisture readings at the start and each visit, a written scope of work and invoices for emergency services.

Emergency water removal and water mitigation are normally billed separately from repairs, which helps the adjuster see what was done to limit the loss. We never promise an insurance outcome and we do not bill insurers directly.

Mistakes that complicate a claim

  • Throwing away damaged items before photographing them
  • Waiting days to report because the leak seemed small
  • Letting temporary protection lapse, such as a tarp on a roof opening
  • Skipping receipts for emergency work and extra living costs
  • Accepting a verbal explanation and not asking for the reason in writing

If you believe a claim was handled unfairly, you can contact the California Department of Insurance, which handles consumer questions about insurers. Keep your log of calls and letters handy, because dates and names help.

None of this means a claim will be approved. It means you give your insurer what it needs to make a decision, and you protect yourself if you need to ask questions later.

Keeping a clean claim file

Use one folder, paper or digital, for the whole claim. Put in it the policy declarations page, photos, the written log of calls, every letter and email, invoices and receipts, and notes from each conversation with the adjuster. Add the date to every item.

Send documents in a way that leaves a record, such as email or a claim portal. If something is said on a call, follow up with a short written summary. A tidy file makes every later step faster, including a second look at a decision you disagree with.

Questions to put to your insurer

  • Is this loss treated as sudden and accidental under my policy?
  • What is my deductible for water damage?
  • Does my policy cover the cost to tear out and replace the damaged materials?
  • Is sewer or drain backup covered, and by what endorsement?
  • Are additional living expenses included, and what documents do you need?
  • What are the deadlines for reporting and for proof of loss?

Frequently asked questions

Does house insurance cover water damage from a burst pipe?

It often does when the damage is sudden and accidental, but your policy language and exclusions decide. Report it quickly and ask your insurer.

Is a slow leak covered?

Gradual leaks and damage from neglect are often excluded. Fixing leaks promptly and keeping maintenance records helps.

Is flood covered by standard house insurance?

Usually not. Flood needs a separate policy, and the California Department of Insurance says it takes effect 30 days after signing.

Should I wait for the adjuster before cleaning up?

No. Take photos, then mitigate to prevent further damage. Waiting can increase the loss and may affect coverage.

Does sewage backup count as water damage on my policy?

It depends on your coverage. Many policies need an endorsement for backup, so ask your insurer how a sewer or septic backup would be treated.
