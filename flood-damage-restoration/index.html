If water is still entering, stay out of it. Moving cars and stored belongings to higher ground helps if you can do it safely.

When flood water is an emergency

Flood water is classed as contaminated until proven otherwise. Runoff picks up soil, fuel, fertilizer and animal waste, and sewage backups are a hazard from the first minute. That is why flood jobs are treated as Category 2 or Category 3 water under the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard, and why porous materials are usually removed instead of dried.

Speed matters for two reasons. Water sitting in drywall and pad keeps spreading, and contaminated materials get worse the longer they stay wet. If the flood is in your living space or touches your HVAC ducts, call at night and on weekends without waiting for business hours.

Stay out of the water and call the right people first

Do not walk through flood water that could be energized, do not run a household vacuum on it and do not mix cleaning chemicals. Call the utility or 911 for downed lines, gas smell or a wet electrical panel before calling the restoration line.

What floods Apple Valley homes

The Mojave River borders the west side of town, and desert washes and low-lying lots carry runoff when it rains hard. Heavy rain is not frequent here, which means drainage, roofs and block walls are tested rarely. Tropical Storm Hilary in 2023 brought torrential rainfall to mountain and desert communities across Southern California, according to the National Weather Service.

Flood risk is specific to your address, and a neighbor a few streets away can be in a different zone. Check the FEMA flood map for your address at the Flood Map Service Center, and ask the Town of Apple Valley about drainage rules for your lot. We do not guess your zone from the street name.

Not every flood is weather. A failed sump line, a hose left running, a fire sprinkler line or a main supply break can put as much water into a house as a storm. The cleanup steps are the same, but the water category may be cleaner, and the insurance answer can be different.

Basement and lower-level flooding cleanup

Whether your home has a basement, a sunken room, a finished garage or just a slab, the lowest point is where water gathers. Lower levels are slow to dry because they get less airflow and the surrounding soil can keep feeding moisture into the walls.

A basement or lower-level job starts by pumping out water in stages so the structure is not stressed, then removing wet carpet, pad and drywall to above the water line, cleaning and sanitizing the framing and slab, and drying with dehumidifiers and air movers. Wet insulation is removed because it holds water against the wall.

Crawl spaces get their own mention. Water in a crawl space is easy to miss because no one goes under there. It can wet the floor system above and feed mold on the underside of the subfloor. A crew pumps the water, checks the vapor barrier and insulation and dries the framing before anything is closed up.

Restoration is not waterproofing. We dry and repair what the flood damaged, but we do not install drainage or foundation waterproofing. If water keeps coming back, the right next step is a drainage or foundation specialist, and we will say so.

Sewage backup cleanup

Sewage backup is Category 3 water, and safety rules come first. Stay out, shut off the water to the affected fixture, keep children and pets away and leave fans off, since airflow spreads contamination through the house.

Where the water came from matters. The Town of Apple Valley utilities element says the Town operates the local sewer collection system and sends wastewater to the Victor Valley Wastewater Reclamation Authority plant in Victorville, and that development outside the sewer is served by septic systems. A blockage in the Town line and a full septic tank are handled by different people, so find out which one you have.

A crew removes contaminated water and every porous material it touched, cuts drywall above the wick line, cleans and disinfects hard surfaces and runs air scrubbers during the work. Carpet, pad, insulation and particle board that took sewage water are discarded.

How flood restoration is carried out

  1. Safety check. Power, gas and structural concerns are handled before crews enter.
  2. Extraction. Pumps and truck-mounted or portable extractors remove standing water.
  3. Tear-out. Porous materials touched by contaminated water are removed and bagged.
  4. Cleaning and sanitizing. Hard surfaces are cleaned, then treated with an antimicrobial matched to the material.
  5. Structural drying. Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the framing and slab with logged readings.
  6. Repair. After the dry standard is reached, water damage repair rebuilds what was removed.

Drying follows water mitigation practice, and odors or growth that develop afterward are covered under mold remediation.

Frequently asked questions

Do you handle flood damage emergencies at night or on weekends in Apple Valley?

Yes. Dispatch is open 24 hours a day, every day, and a crew is sent the same day when one is available. Flood and sewage calls are treated as emergencies at any hour.

Is my homeowners insurance going to cover a flood?

Standard homeowners policies often exclude flood, and separate flood insurance has a 30 day waiting period according to the California Department of Insurance. Water from a burst pipe or appliance is often handled differently, so check your policy.

How do I find out whether my home is in a flood zone?

Use the FEMA Flood Map Service Center with your address, and ask the Town of Apple Valley about local drainage. We do not assign a zone from memory.

Can flood-damaged drywall and carpet be saved?

After contaminated water, porous materials are usually removed. Some hard surfaces and framing can be cleaned and dried, which is why an assessment comes first.

Do you fix drainage so the flooding does not come back?

No. Restoration repairs damage and dries the structure. Drainage, grading and waterproofing are specialty work, and we say so honestly.

Does sewage backup count as a flood?

It is handled like one because the water is Category 3. Insurance may treat sewer backup differently from flood, so ask your insurer about backup coverage.
